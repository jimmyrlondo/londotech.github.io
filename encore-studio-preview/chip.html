      What to make

      Every stem starts at the very first sample and runs the full length of the song, so they line up when you drop them into any DAW. Played together they add up exactly to the mix.

      What this is

      A chiptune maker with a piano roll on top of real sound-chip voices. The 2A03 has two pulse channels, a stepped triangle, a noise channel and a channel for 1-bit drum samples (8-bit console style). The DMG has two pulses, a 4-bit wave channel and noise, in stereo (8-bit handheld style).

      Chip mode and Free mode

      Chip mode (on when you start) keeps the song to what the real chip can do: its channels, its four pulse widths, 16 volume steps, its note range and pitch steps, no volume on the triangle. If you try something the chip can't do, the line under the piano roll tells you why. The badge says "Hardware-legal" while the whole song fits. Free mode lifts the limits (extra channels, any pulse width, smooth volume, exact pitch) and the badge lists what a real chip couldn't play. Switching back to Chip mode fixes the song for you and tells you what changed.

      Writing a song

      Pick a channel above the piano roll, then click to add notes and drag to stretch them. A song is a list of rows; each row picks a pattern for every channel, so a drum pattern can repeat while the melody changes. Use the row buttons to move between rows, and the Song tab to arrange them.

      The effects

      Arpeggio flips through a chord very fast, the classic way to play a chord on one channel. Vibrato wobbles the pitch in steps, the way a sound driver does it. Slide bends the note; glide slides in from the last note. The volume envelope shapes each note, and duty sweep changes the pulse width as the note plays. Everything updates 60 times a second, like the drivers in old games.

      Your songs

      Everything runs on your device. Songs are saved in this browser, and you can download a song file to keep or share. Nothing is uploaded. The sounds are made from scratch by the chip models; no game sounds are used.
