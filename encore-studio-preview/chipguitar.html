    Pulse and triangle are 2A03-style voices (8-bit console style), the wave voice is a DMG-style 32-step 4-bit wave. They play on the chips' own pitch steps and 16 volume steps.

    Tracking

    At 100% every note snaps to the nearest semitone, the classic chip sound. Lower it to follow bends and vibrato from your hands.

    Arpeggio

    Play one note and the chip plays the chord as a fast arpeggio. "In key" picks the chord that belongs to that note in your key, so A in C major gives A minor.

    What it does

    Chip Guitar listens to one note at a time from your guitar, works out its pitch, and plays that pitch on sound-chip voices: pulse waves, the stepped triangle, a 4-bit wave and noise. Turn up the arpeggio and one note becomes a whole chord played the chiptune way.

    How it hears your notes

    Two trackers work together. A fast one answers about one and a half cycles after you pick (around 18 ms on the low E string, 3 ms up the neck). A careful one checks two full cycles and locks the exact note (about 26 ms on low E). Quantize amount blends the two: at 100% you get clean chip notes, lower values follow bends.

    Latency

    The panel shows what it measured on your last note: how fast each tracker answered, plus the delay of your audio interface and browser. Low strings always take longer, because one cycle of low E alone lasts 12 ms. Play single notes; chords confuse any one-note tracker.

    Tips

    • Use headphones. Speakers near the mic make the effect hear itself.
    • Neck pickup, tone rolled back a little, no distortion before it.
    • If notes cut out, lower the gate. If it chirps between notes, raise it.
    • Wired headphones and an audio interface keep the delay low. Bluetooth adds a lot.
