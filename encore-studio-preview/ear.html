        How it works

        Each question plays something, you answer with a button, a key or your voice, and you find out at once why it was right or wrong. On a miss you can hear your answer and the right one back to back.

        The five drills

        Scale degrees (the best place to start): you hear the key, then one note, and name its number in the scale. After you answer, the note walks home so you feel its pull. This is how most musicians learn to hear melodies.

        Intervals: the distance between two notes, rising, falling or together. Chords: major, minor and more. Progressions: name the chords after I (home). Melody: write a short tune by its scale numbers.

        It adapts to you

        Every item (degree 7, a minor 6th, the IV chord) keeps its own score. Ones you miss come back sooner, ones you know come back less, and when you mix two up they come back as a pair. You start with a few; a new one joins once you're getting the others right about 9 times in 10.

        Singing an answer

        Press "Sing it" and sing the note you heard (any octave). Scale degrees and melodies: sing the note. Intervals: sing the second note. Hold it steady for a moment. Headphones help.
