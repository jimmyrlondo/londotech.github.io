      What this makes

      A voicebank: one short recording of every sound in a language, plus a timing file (oto.ini) that tells singing software where each sound starts. OpenUtau, the free singing program, stitches the sounds together to sing any melody and lyrics you type.

      Why the note never changes

      Every sound in the list is sung on the same note, on purpose. The singing software bends each recording to whatever melody it sings, and it sounds most natural when every sample starts from one steady pitch. To change the note, pick a different one in Setup. Recording the whole list again on a second note (a few steps higher or lower) makes the voice sound better across a wider range.

      Hearing an example

      "Hear an example" says the sound with your device's Japanese speaking voice, so you can copy how it's pronounced. Copy the sound, not its pitch: then sing it on your note. If your device has no Japanese voice the button is greyed out (Windows: Settings, Time & language, Speech, add Japanese).

      Recording English

      Pick "English (ARPAsing)" under Sound list in Setup. Each English line is a short phrase like "bah boo beeb": sing one syllable on each beat, smoothly and all on the note, and the syllable you're on lights up orange. The recorder cuts every line into the joins between its sounds ("b" into "ah", "ah" into "b"), which is what OpenUtau's English mode stitches words from. The zip is set up so OpenUtau picks its English (Arpasing) phonemizer automatically: type normal English lyrics. "Hear an example" uses your device's English voice. You can record Japanese and English into the same voicebank.

      How a take works

      Press Record. You hear three clicks with the note playing, then the orange light comes on: sing the sound once, right on that beat, and hold the vowel steady until the light goes off. The recorder checks the take (volume, noise, timing, pitch, length) and sets the timing markers for you. You can drag a marker on the waveform if it's off.

      For the best voice

      • Wear headphones so the clicks and the note don't get into the microphone.
      • Find the quietest room you can. Turn off fans and air conditioning.
      • Stay the same distance from the mic every time (about a hand's width).
      • Keep the same voice and energy for the whole list. Short breaks are fine; the list is saved as you go.
      • Wireless headphones add delay. Wired ones keep the count-in tight.
