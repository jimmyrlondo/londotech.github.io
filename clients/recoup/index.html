Site review

What we found on the site

The practice has real strengths: 18 clinicians, most accepting new clients, a wide range of specialties, and solid location pages for Medina and Dublin. The gaps are almost all in the last step, where an interested visitor tries to become a client.

FindingWhy it costs clients
Biggest issue

Every “Schedule” button opens an email draft, not a form or booking calendar.

On phones and work computers this often opens nothing or the wrong app. People give up silently, and there is no way to count how many tried.

Privacy

The email template asks for name, date of birth, phone and insurance.

That is health-adjacent personal data going through regular email. It is also a lot to type before anyone has answered a single question.

No insurance list on the site.

“Do you take my insurance?” is the first question most people have. With no answer, they move on to a practice that shows one.

No reviews or trust signals on the homepage.

Visitors have nothing to reassure them before reaching out. Counselors cannot ask current clients for testimonials, so Google reviews and credentials do this job.

One phone number (330, Medina area code) for both offices.

A Dublin searcher sees a Northeast Ohio number. A local Columbus number would build trust and help local search.

Willoughby and telehealth-only clinicians are listed, but there is no Willoughby page.

Searches near Willoughby, and statewide telehealth searches, have nowhere to land.

“Join the Team” and “Therapist Safety Group” sit in the main menu.

Some of the traffic may be job seekers and clinicians, not prospective clients, which would inflate the visitor count.

Waitlisted clinicians, including the clinical director, use the same booking buttons.

A client who picks a waitlisted therapist may not hear back quickly and assume the practice is full.

The plan

Fix the front door, then win local search, then build authority

More traffic is wasted until phase one is done. Each phase builds on the one before it.

Phase 1 · First 30 days

Fix the front door and start measuring

  • Connect Google Analytics and Google Search Console to see real visitors, what they searched, and where they drop off.
  • Replace the email “Schedule” buttons with one secure request form, ideally the practice’s own scheduling system’s booking widget.
  • Add an insurance and fees page, and answer it in one line on the homepage.
  • Make “accepting new clients” obvious, and route waitlisted clinicians to a “match me with someone available” option.
  • Agree on a response-time promise (for example, one business day) and who owns the inbox.
Phase 2 · Months 1 to 3

Win local search

  • Claim, verify and fully fill out a Google Business Profile for each office: categories, services, hours, photos.
  • Start a simple, ethical review routine (former clients and referral partners, never current clients asked directly).
  • Make name, address and phone match everywhere: the site, Google, Psychology Today and other directories.
  • Add pages for Willoughby and Ohio-wide telehealth, plus service pages that pair a specialty with a city, like “child therapy in Medina.”
Phase 3 · Months 3 to 6+

Build authority

  • Turn the blog toward the questions local people actually search, each post linking to the matching service page.
  • Strengthen therapist bios as search pages: specialties, insurance, location, and a clear “request this therapist” button.
  • Earn local links from referral partners: schools, pediatricians, OB/GYN offices, foster and adoption agencies.
  • Reconnect Facebook (currently disconnected) and post steadily, pointing back to the site.

Before we start

Questions for the practice

These answers decide where phase one starts. Most take a few minutes.

  • Roughly how many new clients do you start per month today, and how do most of them say they found you?
  • How many emailed appointment requests arrive in a typical week, who answers them, and how fast?
  • Which scheduling or records system do you use (SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, other)? Does it offer a booking form we can put on the site?
  • Which insurance plans do you accept, and what are your self-pay rates?
  • Which clients do you most want more of (children and teens, couples, trauma, perinatal, foster and adoptive families)?
  • Which office has the most open capacity right now: Medina, Dublin, Willoughby, or telehealth?
  • Is Willoughby a permanent location we should promote?
  • Do you have Google Business Profiles for each office, and who has the login?
  • Who has access to the GoDaddy, Google, Facebook and Psychology Today accounts?
  • Is hiring part of the goal? If so, “Join the Team” can stay prominent. If not, it moves to the footer.
  • Is there a monthly budget for ads or tools, or is this effort only?
