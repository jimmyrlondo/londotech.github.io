Meet Micah: Anxiety & Depression Therapist in Dublin, OH

April 24, 2026 · Recoup + Relief

Finding the right therapist isn’t just about learning coping skills. It’s about finding someone who helps you truly understand yourself and create meaningful, lasting change.

At Recoup + Relief Counseling, Micah offers anxiety and depression therapy (in person in Dublin, Ohio and via telehealth throughout the State of Ohio) through a deeply relational, insight-oriented approach. His work goes beyond symptom management, helping clients uncover the underlying patterns that shape their emotional and relational lives.

Looking Beyond Symptoms

Many people come to therapy feeling overwhelmed by anxiety, weighed down by depression, or stuck in patterns they can’t quite explain.

Micah often invites clients to think about these symptoms differently.

Rather than seeing anxiety or depression as the problem itself, he frames them more like a fever, a signal that something deeper is going on beneath the surface.

Coping skills, behavioral strategies, and tools can absolutely help reduce distress, and for some, that’s enough. But Micah’s work is centered on helping clients understand why those symptoms are there in the first place.

Together, you’ll begin to explore:

● What makes you vulnerable to these patterns

● What might be unintentionally maintaining them

● How your internal world and relationships shape your experience

The goal isn’t just relief. It’s lasting change.

Therapy as a Real Relationship

Micah’s approach is rooted in the belief that many of our struggles are relational at their core.

How we connect, protect ourselves, avoid, or seek closeness often develops in early relationships, and continues to show up in our present lives.

That’s why the therapy relationship itself becomes an important part of the work.

As you talk, patterns don’t just get discussed. They begin to show up in real time. Micah gently brings attention to these moments, creating opportunities to understand and experience yourself differently, right there in session.

This kind of work can feel deeper than traditional “problem-solving” therapy. For many clients, it’s also what makes therapy feel more meaningful and more effective.

Understanding the Past Without Getting Stuck There

Exploring your past can be an important part of therapy but only insofar as it helps you understand your present.

Many people already have a sense that their early relationships shaped them (“I get that from my mom” or “that’s just how I am”). Micah helps turn that awareness into something more useful: insight that leads to freedom.

As he often puts it, the goal is to: “Gain freedom from the past to live more fully in the present.”

Who Micah Works Best With

Micah primarily works with older adolescents (16+) and adults who are looking for more than just surface-level change.

He is a strong fit for clients who are:

● Struggling with anxiety or chronic stress

● Experiencing depression or emotional heaviness

● Noticing repetitive relationship patterns

● Dealing with personality-related patterns (including perfectionism, rigidity, or obsessive-compulsive personality traits)

● Feeling stuck, disconnected, or unsure why things aren’t improving

He also has a depth of experience working with professionals in high-responsibility roles, including healthcare providers, attorneys, and other high-performing individuals navigating stress, burnout, or identity-related challenges.

A Thoughtful, Integrative Approach

Micah’s work is grounded in psychodynamic and relational therapy, while also integrating practical tools when helpful.

For example:

● Insight-oriented exploration helps uncover deeper patterns

● Behavioral strategies can reduce immediate distress

● When needed, structured approaches (like CBT) are incorporated to challenge unhelpful thinking

This balance allows therapy to be both deeply reflective and practically useful.

Therapy That Creates Real Change

People often start therapy hoping to feel better.

Micah’s work goes a step further, helping you understand yourself in a way that changes how you relate to your thoughts, emotions, and relationships over time.

For many clients, this leads not only to symptom relief, but to a deeper sense of clarity, stability, and self-understanding.
