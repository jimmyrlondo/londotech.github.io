Back-to-School Anxiety: Helping Your Child Feel Confident

August 10, 2026 · Recoup + Relief

As summer comes to a close, many children and teens begin thinking about returning to school. While some look forward to seeing friends and getting back into a routine, others experience worry, nervousness, or even fear. A little anxiety is a normal part of adjusting to change. However, when those feelings begin affecting your child's sleep, mood, behavior, or daily life, it may be a sign they need additional support.

At Recoup + Relief Counseling, we work with children, teens, and families throughout Dublin and Medina, Ohio, helping them build healthy coping skills and confidence during life's transitions, including the start of a new school year.

What Is Back-to-School Anxiety?

Back-to-school anxiety is the stress or worry some children and teens experience before or during the transition back to school. It can affect students of any age and may stem from academic pressure, social concerns, changes in routine, or previous negative school experiences.

For some children, these feelings are temporary. For others, anxiety may become overwhelming and interfere with everyday life.

Signs Your Child May Be Struggling

Every child expresses anxiety differently. Some are very open about their worries, while others show signs through changes in behavior.

Common signs of back-to-school anxiety include:

  • Frequent stomachaches or headaches before school
  • Trouble sleeping or nightmares
  • Increased irritability or emotional outbursts
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Avoiding conversations about school
  • Excessive worrying about grades or friendships
  • Clinginess or reluctance to separate from parents
  • Changes in appetite
  • Withdrawing from activities they normally enjoy

If these behaviors continue for several weeks or begin impacting your child's daily functioning, it may be time to seek additional support.

Common Causes of School Anxiety

There isn't one single reason children experience back-to-school anxiety. Often, several factors contribute to how they're feeling.

Academic Pressure

Some students worry about keeping up with coursework, meeting expectations, or adjusting to a more challenging grade level.

Social Concerns

Friendships change, new classmates join, and social dynamics shift every year. Children and teens may worry about fitting in, making friends, or feeling accepted.

Big Changes

Starting kindergarten, middle school, high school, or attending a new school can create uncertainty, even for confident children.

Previous Difficult Experiences

Children who have experienced bullying, learning challenges, or difficult classroom experiences may feel especially anxious about returning.

Changes at Home

Family transitions such as divorce, moving, welcoming a new sibling, or other life changes can increase stress and make school feel even more overwhelming.

How Parents Can Help

While you can't remove every stressful situation, there are many ways you can help your child feel supported.

Start Talking Early

Begin conversations about school before the first day arrives. Ask open-ended questions such as:

  • What are you looking forward to?
  • Is there anything you're feeling nervous about?
  • What would help you feel more prepared?

Listening without immediately trying to solve the problem allows your child to feel heard and understood.

Rebuild Routines

Gradually returning to school routines before classes begin can ease the transition.

Focus on:

  • Earlier bedtimes
  • Consistent wake-up times
  • Regular meals
  • Reduced screen time before bed
  • Daily routines that create predictability

Children often feel more secure when they know what to expect.

Validate Their Feelings

Avoid saying things like:

  • "There's nothing to worry about."
  • "You'll be fine."
  • "Everyone feels that way."

Instead, acknowledge their emotions.

You might say:

"I can see you're feeling nervous about going back to school. That makes sense. Let's talk about what's worrying you."

Validation helps children feel safe expressing their emotions.

Focus on What They Can Control

Help your child identify small actions they can take to build confidence.

Examples include:

  • Packing their backpack the night before
  • Picking out clothes ahead of time
  • Walking through their morning routine
  • Practicing introductions if they're attending a new school
  • Identifying one trusted adult they can talk to during the school day

Small successes often reduce anxiety over time.

When Anxiety May Be More Than Normal

Some nervousness before school is expected. However, if anxiety becomes persistent or begins interfering with your child's daily life, professional support may be beneficial.

Consider speaking with a therapist if your child:

  • Frequently refuses to attend school
  • Experiences panic attacks
  • Has ongoing physical symptoms related to anxiety
  • Withdraws from friends or family
  • Shows significant changes in mood or behavior
  • Struggles with anxiety beyond the first few weeks of school

Early support can help children develop healthy coping skills before anxiety becomes more difficult to manage.

How Therapy Can Help

Therapy provides children and teens with a safe, supportive environment to better understand their emotions and develop practical strategies for managing anxiety.

Depending on your child's needs, therapy may focus on:

  • Identifying anxious thoughts
  • Building confidence
  • Learning healthy coping skills
  • Managing stress
  • Improving emotional regulation
  • Strengthening communication
  • Supporting parents with strategies to use at home

Every child is different, and therapy is tailored to meet them where they are.

Supporting Your Child Through a Successful School Year

Back-to-school season is filled with change, and it's normal for children and teens to need extra reassurance during this transition. By recognizing the signs of anxiety early and providing consistent support, you can help your child build confidence and resilience throughout the school year.

If you're concerned that your child's anxiety is becoming difficult to manage, you don't have to navigate it alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal for kids to have anxiety before school starts?

Yes. Many children experience some nervousness before returning to school. Anxiety becomes more concerning when it significantly affects sleep, behavior, relationships, or school attendance.

How do I know if my child needs therapy for anxiety?

If anxiety is persistent, interferes with daily life, or continues beyond the normal adjustment period, speaking with a mental health professional can help determine the best next steps.

Do you provide therapy for teens?

Yes. Recoup + Relief Counseling offers therapy for children, teens, adults, couples, and families. Our therapists work with clients experiencing anxiety, depression, trauma, ADHD, relationship concerns, life transitions, and more.

Do you offer therapy in Dublin and Medina?

Yes. We provide in-person counseling services in both Dublin and Medina, Ohio, as well as telehealth appointments for many clients.
