This is not a political post.

July 10, 2026 · Recoup + Relief

As June comes to a close and Pride celebrations wind down, it’s worth remembering that LGBTQIA+ inclusion isn’t seasonal. It’s about people: our friends, neighbors, co‑workers, and loved ones. Sometimes we know someone’s sexual orientation or gender identity, and sometimes we don’t. That uncertainty may stem from personal privacy, safety concerns, or past experiences with discrimination. What matters is how we show up for others, every month of the year.

Research continues to highlight the disproportionate mental health challenges faced by LGBTQIA+ individuals. According to a study done at the University of Michigan, individuals that identify as LGBTQIA+ are 2.5x more likely to experience anxiety and/or depression. In addition, the Trevor Project reports 36-40% of young LGBTQIA+ individuals have seriously considered suicide compared to the general population at 14% and 10-12%have attempted suicide where the general population is 6%. A study on health policy by UCLA shows these numbers are even greater among the Bisexual and Trans communities.

These numbers reflect lived experiences shaped by minority stress, discrimination, bullying, family rejection, political hostility, and religious persecution.

Creating safer, more affirming environments doesn’t require grand gestures. It requires consistent, respectful behavior. Here are practical ways to support the people around us:

  • Avoid assumptions about someone’s sexual orientation, gender identity, or relationship structure.
  • Use the names and pronouns people ask you to use. Follow their lead.
  • Reject discriminatory language and speak up when others use jokes, slurs, or derogatory comments.
  • Ask respectful questions when you’re unsure. Curiosity is okay when paired with care.
  • Show genuine care through your everyday interactions.

You may never know when someone is struggling. That’s why respect and empathy must be our default. When we create environments where people feel safe, valued, seen, and cared about, we give them space to show up as their full authentic selves.
