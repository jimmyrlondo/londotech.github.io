One of the most common charges, with lasting consequences

Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs is one of the most common criminal charges in the U.S. Even one drink can be enough to put some people over Ohio’s legal limit of 0.08% blood alcohol content (BAC). A conviction can affect your license, your job and your family for years.

Calling our office is the first step to getting back to your day-to-day life. We have experience defending arrests with serious circumstances, including personal injury, property damage and other liabilities.

OVI, DUI, DWI: what’s the difference?

DUI (driving under the influence), DWI (driving while impaired), OMVI (operating a motor vehicle impaired) and OVI (operating a vehicle impaired) all describe essentially the same offense. Ohio law uses “OVI.”

Ohio no longer requires the vehicle to be motorized, so you can be charged for riding a bicycle while impaired. There is also a physical control charge, which covers simply being in the driver’s seat of a vehicle while impaired. The vehicle does not have to be moving or even running. This charge often comes up when someone tries to “sleep it off” rather than drive.

A working knowledge of current OVI law and a thorough consultation let us pursue options such as reduced charges (for example, reckless operation), driving privileges and shorter suspensions.

Every arrest is different

Police must follow specific procedures during an OVI arrest. The reason for the stop, how field sobriety tests were given, and whether every step was done correctly can have a major impact on your case. The law is not always black and white, and there are laws in place to protect you too.

The sooner you contact us, the more we can do. Once we learn the details of your arrest, we can give you the answers you need to start moving forward.
