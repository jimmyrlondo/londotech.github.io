Aggressive representation starts with you

Being charged with a criminal offense is serious. Taking a proactive approach by talking with an experienced criminal defense lawyer is step one. A consultation gives you direction and can help you avoid mistakes that could hurt your defense.

Your initial phone consultation is free, and we will give you our best advice to protect you and your rights under the law, for any criminal matter in Lorain County, Cuyahoga County or anywhere in Ohio.

Misdemeanor or felony?

Whether a charge is a misdemeanor or a felony depends on how serious it is, which is usually measured by the potential jail time.

In Ohio, a misdemeanor carries a potential sentence of up to 180 days in jail. Trespassing and vandalism are common examples, and plea negotiations are common. Our attorneys can advise you through that process.

A felony carries a potential prison sentence of six months or more, and covers offenses such as burglary, arson and serious violent crimes. If you have been charged with a felony, talk to an attorney immediately.

Judicial release (shock probation)

Even after a prison sentence, a person may be eligible for early release, known as judicial release or shock probation. Contact our office to find out whether this option applies.

Possession vs. trafficking

Trafficking means selling, manufacturing, growing or delivering illegal drugs. Possessing more than a set amount can also be charged as trafficking, depending on the substance. Your criminal history, the substances involved and the amounts all matter, and either charge can be a felony. We provide aggressive representation on all drug charges.

Expungement & record sealing

Clearing an old record can be life changing. Ohio overhauled its record sealing and expungement law in 2023 (Senate Bill 288). The law now treats sealing (hiding a record from the public) and expungement (permanently destroying it) as separate options, and shortened many waiting periods. Call for a free consultation to see whether you qualify.

What does aggressive representation mean?

It means a steadfast respect for due process. Due process holds the government to the same law as its citizens and protects individuals from the state. When the government harms a person without following the exact course of the law, that is a due process violation. Aggressive legal representation is a passion for that principle.

We believe in your right to be presumed innocent until proven guilty, and we support the Innocence Project, a nonprofit committed to exonerating wrongly convicted people, and the National Registry of Exonerations, which tracks every known exoneration in the U.S. since 1989.

Legal terms

Indictment
A written accusation that a person committed, or failed to do, something punishable by law.
Arraignment
The hearing where the defendant is formally told the charges and asked to plead guilty, not guilty or no contest. Bail or release may also be decided here.
Hearing
A proceeding where evidence is presented to decide an issue of law or fact.
Dismissal
The State drops the charges, for lack of evidence, an error in law or a plea agreement. “With prejudice” means they can never be refiled; “without prejudice” means they might be.
Acquittal
A formal finding that the accused is not guilty of the charge.
Reversal
A court of appeals ruling that a lower court’s judgment was wrong. The lower court may be told to dismiss, retry or change its judgment.
