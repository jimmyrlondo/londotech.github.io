Direction when your family is in crisis

Family law is one of the most difficult areas of the law. The process is often emotional, and the issues can be complex. We provide services in all areas of family law and will guide you through it with dedication and compassion.

We understand the stress of ending a relationship. We work to move proceedings along and negotiate with strength and determination, so you can move forward to the next chapter of your life.

Legal separation

In Ohio, the person filing for divorce must have lived in the state for at least six months before filing. If you don’t meet that requirement yet, or you aren’t sure you want to end the marriage permanently, a legal separation can settle spousal support, custody and visitation, child support, property division and payment of marital debts in the meantime. Some people also choose legal separation for religious reasons.

A separation agreement can later be converted into a divorce or dissolution.

Dissolution vs. divorce

A dissolution is often called a no-fault divorce. Both spouses are “petitioners” rather than plaintiff and defendant, and it can only be filed once you have agreed on every issue: property, custody, support and the rest. Because everything is settled up front, it is usually worth having an attorney protect your interests in the agreement. A domestic relations attorney represents you the same way in a dissolution as in a divorce.

In a divorce, one spouse files to end the marriage and must state legal grounds. Ohio allows a divorce for these reasons:

  • Incompatibility (unless the other spouse denies it)
  • Living separate and apart for one year
  • Adultery
  • Extreme cruelty
  • Gross neglect of duty
  • Habitual drunkenness
  • Willful absence for one year
  • Imprisonment of the other spouse
  • Fraudulent contract
  • Bigamy

Temporary orders can protect you while the divorce is pending. These can cover the residential parent and parental rights for minor children, child support, spousal support, and attorney fees and litigation expenses.

Shared parenting

A shared parenting agreement sets the rights and responsibilities of both parents who want an active role in raising their children in separate households. It gives everyone a consistent set of rules, makes the transition easier for parents and children, and encourages parents to work together for their children’s well-being.

Civil protection orders & domestic violence

A civil protection order (CPO) is a court order directing a person to have no contact with the protected person or people. It is separate from a divorce case and can last up to five years. Violating a CPO can lead to criminal charges, and police must enforce it. A CPO can also remove the abuser from the home, award temporary custody or support, and require the abuser to stay at least 500 feet from the victim’s home or workplace.

During a divorce, the court may instead issue a restraining order to protect one spouse from the other. It lasts while the case is pending. Police do not enforce restraining orders; the protected spouse files a motion for contempt in the same court.
