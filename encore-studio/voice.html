Sing anything

Your pitch draws on the note lanes as you sing. The line is green when you're within 15 cents of a note (a cent is a hundredth of a semitone), yellow when you're close, red when you're between notes.

Try a scale against a drone on the key note: it's one of the best ways to train your ear (best with headphones, so the microphone hears only you). A slow, gentle "ah" gives the clearest reading.

Find your range

Three short steps, about a minute in all. Use an easy "ah" and never force a note: the comfortable edges are what we're after.

  1. 1
    Low. Start on a comfortable middle note and slide slowly down to the lowest note you can make without growling or pushing. Hold it for a moment.
  2. 2
    High. Start in the middle again and slide up as high as feels easy. Light, breathy or falsetto notes count. A siren (up and down) works too.
  3. 3
    Comfortable. Sing something you know well at a relaxed volume, like "Happy Birthday" or a favourite chorus, in whatever key feels natural. This finds where your voice likes to sit.

Warm-up

A guided routine built for your range: hums, lip trills, sirens, arpeggios and long notes, then a cool-down. Each pattern starts low and climbs by half steps, never past your saved range.

Pitch matching

Listen to a note, then sing it back on "ah" or "oo" and hold it. Rounds of 10; it gets harder as you get better, and easier if it needs to.

Scored once the guide stops, so the microphone never mistakes the guide for you. Points: how close (most of it), how quickly you settled, and how steady you held it. A vibrato doesn't count against you.

Long notes: steadiness and vibrato

Hold one note for about six seconds on "ah". You'll see how steady it was, whether it drifted, and your vibrato's speed and width if you have one.

Sing it back

A short tune of two or three notes plays; sing it back on "la". Each note is scored, and so is each jump between notes: the jumps are your ear for intervals, and they count even if you start on a different note.

The best key for a song

Tell me the lowest and highest notes of the melody (or sing it once and I'll catch them), and I'll find the keys that fit your voice.
