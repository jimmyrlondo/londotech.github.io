      How it works

      Play: tap or click the pads (several at once on a phone), or use the keys. Each pad shows its key. A numpad's 7 8 9 / 4 5 6 / 1 2 3 match the pads exactly, and Q W E / A S D / Z X C do the same under your left hand.

      Your own sounds: drop a sound file (WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG, M4A...) onto a pad, or pick a pad and press Load a sound file. Every file you bring in goes into your Library, kept on this device (nothing is uploaded). Beatpad cuts the silence in front of each sound so it plays the instant you hit. In the Pad tab you can set the volume, pitch, pan, start, length and fade, and the choke group.

      Kits are your nine pads: the sounds, their settings and their MIDI notes. Changes save as you go. Built-in kits stay as they are: change one and Beatpad saves your version as a new kit.

      Keys: pick a layout from the Keys list next to the kit: Number pad (7 8 9 / 4 5 6 / 1 2 3), QWE / ASD / ZXC (left hand, any laptop), Number row (1 to 9) or Right hand (U I O / J K L / M , .). A numpad plays the pads whichever you pick (you can turn that off under Keys & MIDI). To make your own, press Map keys, click a pad, then press any key; if another pad had that key, the two swap, and Esc finishes. Your own keys are kept as Custom when you switch to a layout. Keys don't play while you're typing in a box.

      MIDI: press MIDI and allow it. Pads answer to General MIDI drum notes (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat, 46 open hat...), so most drum pads and e-kits just work. Learn notes: hit a pad on screen, then the pad or key on your controller.

      Loop: press Rec, wait out the one-bar count-in, and play. It keeps looping, and every pass adds to it (Undo take removes the last pass). Quantize snaps what you play to the grid. Hold Repeat (or Space) and hold a pad to roll it in time. Save loop as WAV gives you the loop as a file that loops cleanly.

      Share a kit: the .beatpad format

      A .beatpad kit is a normal zip file with a different ending: inside, a kit.json and the sound files. Export kit makes one; you can also make one by hand, or just zip a folder of sounds (no kit.json needed: files go on pads by a number at the front of the name like 01-kick.wav, then by what the name says, like snare.wav, then in order).

      {
  "format": "beatpad", "version": 1,
  "name": "My Kit", "author": "You",
  "pads": [
    { "slot": 1, "name": "Kick", "file": "01-kick.wav",
      "volume": 1, "pitch": 0, "start": 0, "end": null,
      "fade": 0, "pan": 0, "choke": 0,
      "mode": "retrigger", "mute": false,
      "key": "KeyZ", "midi": 36 }
  ]
}
      • slot: 1 to 9, laid out like a numpad (1 2 3 the bottom row, 7 8 9 the top).
      • file: a sound file in the zip, next to kit.json.
      • volume 0 to 2 (1 = as recorded), pitch in semitones (-24 to 24), pan -1 (left) to 1 (right).
      • start and end in seconds into the file (end null = to the end), fade in seconds.
      • choke: 0 for none, or 1 to 8: pads in the same group cut each other off.
      • mode: "retrigger" (a new hit restarts it) or "layer" (hits ring over each other).
      • key: a keyboard key like "KeyZ" or just "z"; midi: a note number 0 to 127.

      Only slot and file are needed; everything else has a default. Your own keys stay yours when you import a kit (you can choose to use the kit's).
