    What this makes

    Learning tracks: one recording per voice part, the way choirs, barbershop quartets and a cappella groups learn new music. In "your part loud" tracks your line is at full volume and everyone else is quieter (and by default your part is in the left ear, the others in the right). "Part missing" tracks leave your line out so you can sing it with the group. The full mix is everyone.

    How to use it

    Open a score, check the part names on the Parts tab, pick your part above and press Play. Slow the tempo down while you learn, loop the hard bars, and use Key note or My first note to find your starting pitch. When it sounds right, the Export tab makes every track in one zip, named like "Song - Tenor predominant.mp3", ready to send to the group.

    Two voices on one staff

    Barbershop and closed choir scores put two parts on one staff (Tenor and Lead, Baritone and Bass). Learning Tracks splits them into separate parts, by voice when the score has two voices, or upper and lower notes when they're written as chords. A staff named "Tenor/Lead" gives its parts those names. Use "Keep together" on the Parts tab if a staff shouldn't be split.

    The sound

    The notes are played by recorded piano, choir "ah" or voice "oo" samples, or a synth voice. These are for learning pitches and rhythms, not a performance: learn the notes here, then learn the music from your director. The tempo control plays the score slower or faster from the notes themselves, so slow copies stay in tune.
